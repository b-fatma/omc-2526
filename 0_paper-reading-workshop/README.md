## The Three-Pass Approach

### First Pass: Quick Scan (5-10 minutes)

**What to do:**
- Read: title, abstract, introduction
- Skim: section headings (ignore everything else)
- Read: conclusions
- Glance: references

**Answer the Five Cs:**
1. **Category** - What type of paper is this?
2. **Context** - How does it relate to other work?
3. **Correctness** - Do the assumptions seem valid?
4. **Contributions** - What's new here?
5. **Clarity** - Is it well written?

**Outcome:** Decide if you need to keep reading

---

### Second Pass: Understand Content (up to 1 hour)

**What to do:**
- Read carefully but skip proofs
- Look closely at figures and graphs
  - Are axes labeled?
  - Are there error bars?
  - Bad figures = rushed work
- Mark interesting references to read later
- Take notes as you go

**Outcome:** You can summarize the main ideas to someone else

**Stuck?** The topic might be new, the writing might be bad, or you might be tired. That's okay, come back later or move to pass three.

---

### Third Pass: Deep Dive (1-5 hours)

**What to do:**
- Try to virtually re-implement the paper
- Challenge every assumption
- Think about how YOU would present each idea
- Jot down future work ideas

**Outcome:** You understand it deeply—strengths, weaknesses, hidden assumptions, everything

**When you need this:** Critical papers for your research or when reviewing

## Quality Metrics: Venues, Researchers, and Papers

### For Venues: Quartile Rankings (Q1-Q4)
- **Q1** (top 25%) → Best quality, strictest peer review
- **Q2-Q3** → Good to moderate quality
- **Q4** (bottom 25%) → Lower impact
- **Check on:** Scimago (scimagojr.com)

### For Papers: Citations
**What are citations?** Number of times other papers reference this work

**Rule of thumb:**
- 100+ citations → Important paper
- 500+ citations → Highly influential
- 1000+ citations → Landmark paper
- **Check on:** Google Scholar

### For Researchers: h-index + Citations
**h-index:** A researcher has h-index of X if they have X papers with at least X citations each

**Example:** h-index of 20 = 20 papers with 20+ citations each

**What's good?**
- h-index 10-20 → Solid researcher
- h-index 20-40 → Established expert
- h-index 40+ → Leading researcher in the field
- **Check on:** Google Scholar profile

## Accessing Paywalled Papers

**DOI (Digital Object Identifier):** Unique ID for each paper (e.g., 10.1145/1273445.1273458)

**Problem:** Many papers are behind paywalls

**Solution:** Sci-Hub (sci-hub.se or other mirrors)
- Paste the DOI or paper URL
- Get free PDF access

## Smart Tools & Systems

### Use Gen AI to Your Advantage

**NotebookLM:** Upload papers → get summaries and audio overviews
- Great for initial understanding
- Audio summaries help if you're good at listening in English
- Use AFTER first pass, not instead of it

### Set Up a Tracking System

**Excel/Notion template with:**
- Title, authors, year, venue
- Summary (in your own words)
- Key findings
- Personal takeaways
- Relevance (use color codes: 🟢 high, 🟡 medium, 🔴 low)

**Why?** You'll forget details over time. Good notes = no re-reading later.

---

## Quick Tips

✅ Don't read papers start to finish—use the three passes

✅ First pass = filter (most papers you'll only do this)

✅ Check publication venue quality on Scimago

✅ High citations = important paper

✅ Survey papers are gold, they summarize entire fields (but you do not need to read all survey papers if many are available)
