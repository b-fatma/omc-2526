# How to Read Research Papers

---

## Why This Matters

Researchers spend hundreds of hours reading papers, but nobody teaches you how to do it efficiently. Let's fix that.

---

## The Three-Pass Approach

### First Pass: Quick Scan (5-10 minutes)

**What to do:**
- Read: title, abstract, introduction
- Skim: section headings (ignore everything else)
- Read: conclusions
- Glance: references

**Answer the Five Cs:**
1. **Category** - What type of paper is this?
2. **Context** - How does it relate to other work?
3. **Correctness** - Do the assumptions seem valid?
4. **Contributions** - What's new here?
5. **Clarity** - Is it well written?

**Outcome:** Decide if you need to keep reading

---

### Second Pass: Understand Content (up to 1 hour)

**What to do:**
- Read carefully but skip proofs
- Look closely at figures and graphs
  - Are axes labeled?
  - Are there error bars?
  - Bad figures = rushed work
- Mark interesting references to read later
- Take notes as you go

**Outcome:** You can summarize the main ideas to someone else

**Stuck?** The topic might be new, the writing might be bad, or you might be tired. That's okay, come back later or move to pass three.

---

### Third Pass: Deep Dive (1-5 hours)

**What to do:**
- Try to virtually re-implement the paper
- Challenge every assumption
- Think about how YOU would present each idea
- Jot down future work ideas

**Outcome:** You understand it deeply—strengths, weaknesses, hidden assumptions, everything

**When you need this:** Critical papers for your research or when reviewing

## Finding Papers: Literature Survey

**Step 1:** Google Scholar → find 3-5 recent papers → read their "Related Work" sections
- **Pro tip:** If you find a survey paper, you're done! Read it and celebrate.

**Step 2:** Notice which papers and authors keep appearing → those are the key ones

**Step 3:** Check where key researchers publish → find the top conferences in that field

**Step 4:** Browse recent proceedings from top conferences → build your reading list

## Publication Venues: Quality Matters

### Quartile Rankings (Q1-Q4)

Venues are ranked by impact:
- **Q1** (top 25%) - Best quality, most prestigious
- **Q2** (25-50%) - Good quality
- **Q3** (50-75%) - Moderate
- **Q4** (bottom 25%) - Lower impact

**Why care?** Q1 papers generally have higher quality and stricter peer review.

### Free Tool: Scimago Journal Rank

**Website:** https://www.scimagojr.com

Search any journal or conference → see its quartile ranking and citation metrics. Completely free.

## AI Publication Venues

### Top AI Conferences (Q1-level)

**Machine Learning:**
- NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR

**Computer Vision:**
- CVPR, ICCV, ECCV

**Natural Language Processing:**
- ACL, EMNLP, NAACL

**General AI:**
- AAAI, IJCAI

**AI Applications:**
- KDD, WWW, SIGIR

### Top AI Journals
- JMLR, PAMI, JAIR, AIJ

### Where to Find AI Papers
- **arXiv.org** - preprints (latest work)
- **Papers with Code** - papers with code
- **Google Scholar** - search and citations
- Conference websites

---

## Quick Tips

✅ Don't read papers start to finish—use the three passes

✅ First pass = filter (most papers you'll only do this)

✅ Check publication venue quality on Scimago

✅ High citations = important paper

✅ Survey papers are gold, they summarize entire fields

✅ In AI, top conferences > many journals

---

## Practice Time

Pick a topic you like → Google Scholar → find 3 papers → do the first pass on each → check venues on Scimago
